Can representations learned for multimodal understanding also be used for image generation?

Integral explores how to leverage pretrained VLM knowledge for image generation through a shared visual representation space.

Part I · How can we share?

Constructing Shared Visual Representations

Motivation

Why Share Visual Representations?

Many unified multimodal models encode visual knowledge for understanding and generation through separate experts modeling different visual representation spaces. However, this representational heterogeneity can make knowledge sharing between these experts challenging.

Sharing visual representations enables effective generative learning that leverages the multimodal knowledge encoded in pretrained VLMs.

We investigate this by constructing a shared visual representation space through Abstraction Fusion (AF). In particular, constructing a shared space for VLMs remains challenging, as pretrained VLM representations often lack the fine-grained information required for image reconstruction. Below, we identify a key bottleneck and introduce AF.

Observation

The Propagation Bottleneck

Challenge: Semantic abstraction vs. pixel reconstruction

Pretrained vision encoders progressively transform low-level visual information into high-level semantic representations. While this abstraction supports visual understanding, the final-layer representations often lack the fine-grained information required for image reconstruction.

One approach is to jointly train the vision encoder for reconstruction. However, this can degrade its pretrained semantic capabilities.

Our hypothesis: The Propagation Bottleneck

When reconstruction relies on the final representation, the encoder may be encouraged to preserve low-level information throughout its depth, interfering with progressive semantic abstraction.

This hypothesis predicts that deeper representations become more similar to the initial-layer representation after joint reconstruction training.

Evidence 1: Changes in representation hierarchy

We measure layer-wise CKA between each encoder layer and the initial layer. After joint training, deeper representations become substantially more similar to the initial-layer representation.

This observation supports our hypothesis, although CKA does not directly measure information propagation.

Evidence 2: Semantics–reconstruction trade-off

During joint training, stronger semantic supervision improves semantic preservation at the expense of reconstruction fidelity, revealing a trade-off between the two. In contrast, AF avoids this trade-off by maintaining the pretrained encoder's semantic abstraction while enabling image reconstruction.

Together, these results are consistent with the propagation bottleneck hypothesis and suggest that preserving the pretrained encoder hierarchy while aggregating representations across depths is key to resolving the semantics–reconstruction trade-off.

Method

Integral

Integral extends a pretrained VLM with image generation through a shared visual representation space. Abstraction Fusion constructs shared visual tokens, while Block-wise MoT generates them using pretrained VLM representations.

Abstraction Fusion: Constructing shared visual representations

Abstraction Fusion (AF) takes the representations at every depth of the frozen vision encoder and fuses them into visual tokens z:

z=AF(h(0),…,h(L))

The tokens play two roles:

s^=gsem(z),x^=gpix(z)

A semantic projector gsem aligns z with the visual input space of the pretrained VLM, and a pixel decoder gpix reconstructs the image from the same z.

By effectively fusing features across the encoder hierarchy, AF reconstructs images at 32.2 dB PSNR while the VLM retains 99.8% of its understanding performance.

Inspecting the learned representation

We examine how AF utilizes representations across encoder depths.

Notably, removing deeper layers reduces reconstruction quality, while removing shallower layers degrades semantic similarity. These results highlight the contributions of different abstraction levels to the shared representation.

Block-wise MoT: A lightweight generative extension

Generating images in a continuous latent space requires the VLM to model noisy latents zt, which it is not pretrained on. To this end, we introduce Block-wise MoT, a lightweight generative module that denoises zt by leveraging pretrained VLM representations.

By incorporating adaLN-based timestep conditioning, our sparse block-wise design achieves generation performance comparable to Full MoT at substantially lower computational cost.

Analysis

Effects of Representation Sharing

AF effectively leverages pretrained multimodal knowledge for image generation. Its strong generation performance emerges under VLM conditioning, but drops under text-only LLM conditioning. In contrast, FLUX.1-VAE shows little benefit from VLM conditioning.

Integrating pretrained VLM representations into a shared visual space is key to leveraging multimodal knowledge for image generation. Comparisons of AF across different variants further support this finding.

What Have We Learned?

  1. Multimodal knowledge acquired through understanding can also be leveraged for image generation. Pretrained VLMs provide a foundation for knowledge-intensive visual generation without learning multimodal knowledge from scratch.
  2. Sharing visual representations is key to transferring this knowledge. A shared visual space allows generation to directly leverage the representations learned for multimodal understanding.
  3. A key bottleneck between image reconstruction and semantic abstraction lies in the encoder layers' capacity for abstraction. Preserving fine-grained details across encoder depth can interfere with the progressive abstraction of visual information.
